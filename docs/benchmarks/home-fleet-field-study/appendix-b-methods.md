# Appendix B. Scored presence methods and reconciliation

A "method" is one distinct scored presence-detector configuration (feature set x model or rule) that produced a detection, false-presence or AUC number on chosen days or windows. Mask and label-free (A) / supervised (B) variants are arms of one row. Dates are dates scored. "dev" days were used for tuning; "held-out" days were scored after the rule was frozen.

## Reconciliation of the "about 27 earlier methods" and the 39-approach inventory

The study's own planning notes mentioned "about 27 earlier presence methods" and, separately, an inventory of 39 approaches. This appendix rebuilds the first list and reports how far the second can be rebuilt.

Scored methods. The rows above number 30 scored methods. Counted as arms (A/B x masked/unmasked, plus the neural-network development configurations) the same record holds at least 60 scored variants. Counting only distinct pre-registered test identifiers gives 12 tests. None of these three counts is 27. One reading gives exactly 27 (the 30 rows minus the three single-window probes, leaving methods scored on one or more day sets) but this is a post hoc interpretation that no source confirms, so the "27" is not reproducible as a stated inventory. The plain statement is: at least 27 distinct scored RF presence detectors, 30 including the single-window probes.

Never scored. The 39-approach inventory itself was recovered only from a session record, not from any durable file. In it, 27 rows were a run subset, 7 were paper-only ideas and 5 were built but unscored. Neither list reproduces the other's "27": the 27 run-subset rows of the inventory and the 27 scored methods rebuilt here are different counts that happen to coincide. From the durable record, 14 items were found that were thought of, planned or built but never scored for presence (firmware port of the presence detector, a header-bit steering option, a room-level activity playback built for display only, three upstream pull requests judged by code tracing only, a whole-capture replay of every family, a burst-rule replay on empty windows, a node-baseline method, a learned-background soak, a still-person later layer, a witness model, phase / angle-of-arrival presence on 2-antenna boards, and fusion of RF with non-RF baselines). The remaining items of the 39 are not identifiable.

Scored but excluded from the 30. Full-rate link statistics (link-level events, no presence rate), feature-family screens (separability tests on 15 empty windows, not detectors), the phone rule and whole-house power (non-RF baselines), a stale adaptive classifier inspection, a design-stage quiescence rule with no false-presence number, the raw-CSI random-convolution probe (run after the inventory, INVALID-DESIGN), room and position studies (not house presence), and a corrected-label re-run of earlier rows (descriptive, no new method).

The "false presence 0.19-0.94" range. The lowest pooled false presence the record accepts is 0.192 (the legacy-frame test, 605 minutes held out). The highest is 0.94 on a single day (supervised arm of the first A/B test, unmasked); the pooled figure for that arm is 0.84-0.89. The range does not hold for every row: some masked label-free arms are lower (0.04-0.15) but the record itself calls those values not real because undecided minutes were counted as absent, and some per-day values exceed 0.94 (up to 1.00). The range is best read as the unmasked pooled or primary-day false presence of the pre-registered series, with the legacy-frame test as floor and the first supervised arm as ceiling; it is not a computed minimum and maximum over every row.
